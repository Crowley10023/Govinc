This file denotes major cornerstones of the security architecture.

1. Every access to backends must provide a CSRF token.
2. Every backend entry point must check authentication and authorization.
3. The assessment direct form may be accessed without authentication or authorization; it therefore uses a unique, obfuscated URL.

4. Roles in the system:
    a) ADMIN: unrestricted access to all functionality and data unless explicitly constrained by deployment policies.
    b) Information Security Manager (ISM): has access to system functionality and data. By default ISM may access everything except items listed under the navigation section "CONFIG"; see exception for Deviation Management below.
    c) Assessment Delegate: may view and edit assessments where they are the assigned user, and may create new assessments. Assessment Delegates may add users to the "users" list of assessments they already have access to (see item 10).
    d) Team Lead (special assignment): a Team Lead may view and edit all assessments for their organisational unit and its child units, and any assessment where they are assigned as user. Team Lead is a special assignment and is not assigned like standard roles; a Team Lead may lead multiple organisational units.
    e) ASSESSOR: may view assessments only where that person is named as the assessment "user".

5. Access rules are implemented in SecurityConfig.java. That class defines which endpoints and resources are accessible to which roles.
6. The system supports three authentication providers: Keycloak, Azure, and local. The local provider is typically used only for the ADMIN account in development environments.
7. Users may have one or more roles. Valid combinations include:
    1. ADMIN and Team Lead
    2. Assessment Delegate and Team Lead
    3. Information Security Manager and Team Lead
8. In production environments the local ADMIN account must be disabled; it is allowed only in development environments (see deployment documentation).
9. User deletion rules: a user may be deleted only when they are not referenced as the "created by" value on an assessment. If a user appears in an assessment's "users" list, they must be removed from those lists before the user can be deleted.

10. Assessment Delegate entitlements:
    a) Assessment Delegates may add users to the "users" list of assessments for which they already have access.

11. Deviation Management (new functionality)
    a) Purpose: Deviation Management records, tracks, reviews, and closes deviations (exceptions) discovered during assessments or operations. It supports creation, assignment, review, approval, mitigation tracking, and closure workflows.
    b) Permissions:
        - ADMIN: full access to Deviation Management (create, view, edit, assign, approve, close).
        - Information Security Manager: full access to Deviation Management (create, view, edit, assign, approve, close).
        - Team Lead: may view and act on deviations related to their organisational unit and its child units (view, comment, propose mitigation). Any additional write/approval rights for Team Lead must be explicitly granted.
        - Assessment Delegate / Assessor: may view deviations related to assessments they have access to and may add comments or propose mitigation actions; creation rights may be granted as needed by policy.
    c) Audit and logging: all actions in Deviation Management (create/modify/assign/approve/close) must be audited with user, timestamp, and before/after state recorded.
    d) Security controls: Deviation Management endpoints must enforce CSRF protection, authentication, and authorization checks consistent with other backend entry points. Sensitive operations should require ISM or ADMIN privileges unless otherwise specified.
